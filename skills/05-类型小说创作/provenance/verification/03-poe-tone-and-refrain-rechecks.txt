329: I had now to combine the two ideas, of a lover lamenting his deceased
330: mistress and a Raven continuously repeating the word “Nevermore.” I had
331: to combine these, bearing in mind my design of varying, at every turn,
332: the application of the word repeated; but the only intelligible mode of
333: such combination is that of imagining the Raven employing the word in
334: answer to the queries of the lover. And here it was that I saw at once
335: the opportunity afforded for the effect on which I had been
336: depending—that is to say, the effect of the variation of application. I
337: saw that I could make the first query propounded by the lover—the first
338: query to which the Raven should reply “Nevermore”—that I could make this
339: first query a commonplace one—the second less so—the third still less,
340: and so on, until at length the lover—startled from his original
341: nonchalance by the melancholy character of the word itself, by its
342: frequent repetition, and by a consideration of the ominous reputation of
343: the fowl that uttered it—is at length excited to superstition, and
344: wildly propounds queries of a far different character—queries whose
345: solution he has passionately at heart—propounds them half in
346: superstition and half in that species of despair which delights in
347: self-torture—propounds them not altogether because he believes in the
348: prophetic or demoniac character of the bird (which, reason assures him,
349: is merely repeating a lesson learned by rote) but because he experiences
350: a phrenzied pleasure in so modeling his questions as to receive from the
351: expected “Nevermore,” the most delicious because the most intolerable of
352: sorrow. Perceiving the opportunity thus afforded me—or, more strictly,
353: thus forced upon me in the progress of the construction—I first
354: established in mind the climax, or concluding query—that query to which
355: “Nevermore” should be in the last place an answer—that in reply to which
356: this word “Nevermore” should involve the utmost conceivable amount of
357: sorrow and despair.

360: all works of art should begin—for it was here, at this point of my
361: pre-considerations, that I first put pen to paper in the composition of
362: the stanza:

364:   “Prophet!” said I, “thing of evil!—prophet still, if bird or devil!
365:   By that heaven that bends above us—by that God we both adore—
366:   Tell this soul with sorrow laden if, within the distant Aidenn,
367:   It shall clasp a sainted maiden whom the angels name Lenore—
368:   Clasp a rare and radiant maiden whom the angels name Lenore.”
369:           Quoth the Raven, “Nevermore.”
370: 
371: I composed this stanza, at this point, first that, by establishing the
372: climax, I might the better vary and graduate, as regards seriousness and
373: importance, the preceding queries of the lover; and, secondly, that I
374: might definitely settle the rhythm, the meter, and the length and
375: general arrangement of the stanza, as well as graduate the stanzas which
376: were to precede, so that none of them might surpass this in rhythmical
377: effect. Had I been able, in the subsequent composition, to construct
378: more vigorous stanzas, I should, without scruple, have purposely
379: enfeebled them, so as not to interfere with the climacteric effect.

472: The effect of the dénouement being thus provided for, I immediately drop
473: the fantastic for a tone of the most profound seriousness—this tone
474: commencing in the stanza directly following the one last quoted, with
475: the line:
476: 
477:   But the Raven, sitting lonely on that placid bust, spoke only, etc.
478: 
479: From this epoch the lover no longer jests—no longer sees anything even
480: of the fantastic in the Raven’s demeanour. He speaks of him as a “grim,
481: ungainly, ghastly, gaunt, and ominous bird of yore,” and feels the
482: “fiery eyes” burning into his “bosom’s core.” This revolution of
483: thought, or fancy, on the lover’s part, is intended to induce a similar
484: one on the part of the reader—to bring the mind into a proper frame for
485: the dénouement—which is now brought about as rapidly and as directly as
486: possible.
