14	IN the happy days when we were young, no description conveyed to us so
15	complete an idea of mysterious reality as that of an Oriental city.  We
16	knew it was actually there, but had such vague notions of its ways and
17	looks!  Let any one remember his early impressions as to Bagdad or Grand
18	Cairo, and then say if this was not so.  It was probably taken from the
19	“Arabian Nights,” and the picture produced was one of strange, fantastic,
20	luxurious houses; of women who were either very young and very beautiful,
21	or else very old and very cunning; but in either state exercising much
22	more influence in life than women in the East do now; of good-natured,
23	capricious, though sometimes tyrannical monarchs; and of life full of
24	quaint mysteries, quite unintelligible in every phasis, and on that
25	account the more picturesque.

45	As soon as we heard the pleasant news--I suppose the news of an
46	engagement ought always to be called pleasant--it was decided that I
47	ought to speak first about it, and speak to the father. We had not been
48	a great while in the neighborhood, and it would look less like a bid
49	for the familiar acquaintance of people living on a larger scale than
50	ourselves, and less of an opening for our own intimacy if they turned
51	out to be not quite so desirable in other ways as they were in the
52	worldly way. For the ladies of the respective families first to offer
53	and receive congratulations would be very much more committing on both
54	sides; at the same time, to avoid the appearance of stiffness, some one
55	ought to speak, and speak promptly. The news had not come to us directly
56	from our neighbors, but authoritatively from a friend of theirs, who was
57	also a friend of ours, and we could not very well hold back. So, in the
58	cool of the early evening, when I had quite finished rasping my lawn
59	with the new mower, I left it at the end of the swath, which had brought
60	me near the fence, and said across it,
61	
62	“Good-evening!”
63	
64	My neighbor turned from making his man pour a pail of water on the earth
65	round a freshly planted tree, and said, “Oh, good-evening! How d'ye do?
66	Glad to see you!” and offered his hand over the low coping so cordially
67	that I felt warranted in holding it a moment.
68	
69	“I hope it's in order for me to say how very much my wife and I are
70	interested in the news we've heard about one of your daughters? May I
71	offer our best wishes for her happiness?”
72	
73	“Oh, thank you,” my neighbor said. “You're very good indeed. Yes, it's
74	rather exciting--for us. I guess that's all for to-night, Al,” he said,
75	in dismissal of his man, before turning to lay his arms comfortably
76	on the fence top. Then he laughed, before he added, to me, “And rather
77	surprising, too.”
